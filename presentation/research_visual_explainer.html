Four clean photos teach a computer to spot a flaw

The task: look at a picture of carpet, leather, tile, grid or wood and point to the exact pixels where something is wrong. This page walks through the idea and the results with pictures.

The idea: judge a patch by comparing it to something

The computer cuts a photo into small squares called patches. For each patch it asks, “does this look normal?” To answer, it needs something to compare against. The two methods compare against different things.

Route A (old method): ask the photo itself

Compare each patch with the rest of the same picture. No extra photos needed, so it is called zero-shot.

Weakness: a big flaw is part of the picture, so it changes what the picture calls “normal.”

Route B (new method): ask four clean photos

Keep a small library of patches from four flaw-free photos. A patch that matches nothing in the library is suspicious.

Strength: the library is never contaminated by the flaw being tested.

What the three scores mean

Pixel AP
How well the highlighted pixels match the real flaw. High means few false alarms and few missed pixels.
AU-PRO
How much of each flaw region is covered. Small flaws count as much as big ones.
Image AUROC
Can it tell a damaged photo from a good one? 100 means perfect.

Where the gain is biggest, and where it is not

Each texture’s flaws were split into the smaller half and the larger half. Green means Route B scored higher on Pixel AP, pink means Route A did.

Median flaw size: Leather 1,232 pixels, Grid 1,586, Carpet 3,982, Wood 7,742, Tile 21,286. This split uses Route B seed 42 only.

How many clean photos does it need?

On carpet, one clean photo already captures most of the gain. Adding more helps only a little, which is why four is a safe, cheap choice.

What this does not show

  • Only three random seeds were used, so the “seed spread” is a rough estimate. Route A uses no reference photos, so it has no spread.
  • The reasons given for where the gain is large or small are hypotheses that fit the numbers. They are not proven.
  • The big-flaw versus small-flaw split uses one seed (42) for Route B.
  • I also tested my own 64-component PCA version of SubspaceAD. It averages 49.7% Pixel AP against 50.0% for Route B, which is essentially a tie. It is my own re-implementation, not the published method.
  • Only the five texture categories were tested, not objects such as bottles or screws.
