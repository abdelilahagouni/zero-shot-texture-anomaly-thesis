Chapter 1

The Broken Floor

Factories make carpets, leather, tiles and wood. Sometimes there is a tiny hole, scratch or stain. We call that a defect.

The robot must point at the defect — but defects are rare, so it has never seen one. It only knows what “normal” looks like. Spooky!

The robot cuts the picture into little squares called patches and gives each patch a weirdness score.

REAL A carpet with a hole. Left: the photo. Middle: where the hole really is. Right: the robot’s weirdness map — red means “weird!”.

Chapter 2

Two detectives

🧐 Detective Mirror (Route A)

“I only look at this floor. A patch is weird if it doesn’t look like the average of the other patches.”

Needs nothing. Can’t cheat. But trusts the floor in front of him.

📸 Detective Photo (Route B)

“I carry 4 photos of a clean floor. A patch is weird if nothing in my photos looks like it.”

Needs 4 clean photos. Never fooled by the floor in front of him.

Both detectives use the same pair of super-glasses (a frozen model called DINOv2). The only difference is who they ask.

🧑‍🔬 Grown-up corner

Route A score = 1 − mean cosine similarity of a patch to all 1,369 patches of the same image. Route B score = 1 − max cosine similarity to the patches of 4 clean reference images. Same DINOv2 ViT-B/14 at 518×518, same smoothing (15×15 Gaussian, σ ≈ 2.6).

🧑‍🔬 Grown-up corner

Toy model: 16 patches, each one number; normal near 0, defect near 1.2. Route A = |x − mean|; Route B = distance to the nearest of 64 clean numbers. In the real data the “contamination index” (distance between the whole-image mean feature and the non-defect mean feature) rises with defect area (pooled Spearman 0.96).

Five-texture averages at the last DINOv2 layer: Pixel AP 32.1 → 50.0, AU-PRO 86.6 → 94.9, image AUROC 87.0 → 99.8. Route B: mean of 3 seeds. 🧑‍🔬 Grown-up corner

Small = defect area at or below the texture’s median. Route B here is seed 42. The more careful test uses all 382 defective pictures: Route B’s advantage grows with the logarithm of defect area (slope difference −2.72 AUROC points per unit, 95% CI [−3.49, −1.93]; −1.1 to −1.7 at intermediate layers). Chapter 6

The Giant and the Middle Kid

Eleven kids stand in a line. Press the button to add grown-up giants. Which one gets bossed around: the average height or the middle kid’s height?

TOY

Detective Mirror trusts the average. A clever published detective named FCA trusts something like the middle kid (the “median”). One giant barely moves the middle kid. But if half the line are giants, even the middle kid gives up — and that is exactly what the FCA authors say happens when a defect covers most of the picture.

🧑‍🔬 Grown-up corner

An analogy, not a proof: FCA compares sorted local feature distributions with a median reference; Route A uses the plain mean of similarities. In our real data, FCA’s size slope (−0.80) is much shallower than Route A’s (−3.89) and not steeper than Route B’s (−1.17); the difference FCA − B is +0.37 [−0.19, +1.08].

With the answer key, about half the trouble on real floors disappears — and about two thirds on the made-up floors. The other half on real floors is still a mystery. Quick fixes that don’t cheat barely help.

🧑‍🔬 Grown-up corner

Route A-clean averages over non-defect patches only (uses the ground-truth mask: a diagnostic). It removes +1.96 of A’s −3.89 size slope on real defects (+1.28 to +2.66) and +5.68 of −8.56 on synthetic ones. At 50% coverage Route A loses 26.1 AUROC points relative to 25% coverage; A-clean loses 0.2. A-median and A-trim recover at most 11%.

Chapter 8

Things that went wrong (we kept them!)

Real scientists keep the failed experiments too. Here are ours:

🚪

The bouncer

We tried a bouncer who picks Detective Mirror or Photo for each picture. The bouncer was bad at it: 26% vs 49% for just always hiring Photo.

🧮

The average trick

The whole-picture average looked great at spotting broken pictures… but our test said “works on only 2 floors”. We didn’t claim it.

🌀

The wobble idea

Features far away wobble more when the defect is big. But the wobble pointed the wrong way to be the cause. Not the explanation.

🏅

Mirror’s tiny win

Detective Mirror beats Photo on small spots only on Leather — by a whisker (0.18 points).

The end

If you remember three things

1️⃣

Photos help. 4 clean photos beat a floor that only looks at itself, on every floor we tried.

2️⃣

Big messes confuse Mirror. A big defect pollutes its own “normal”. Cleaning it fixes half the trouble.

3️⃣

Use the middle kid. A smart single-image detective (FCA) doesn’t fall for the giant.
